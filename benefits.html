Why this matters

What happens to e-waste now

In India about 95 per cent of e-waste workers are outside the regulated system.

They take devices apart by hand with no gloves and no masks.

Source: Rest of World, 2025.

What is inside a broken device

Old electronics contain lead, mercury and cadmium.

Worldwide, badly managed e-waste releases about 58,000 kilograms of mercury a year.

Source: UN Global E-waste Monitor, 2024.

The World Health Organization found children near these sites absorb enough lead and mercury to harm how their brains develop.

Source: World Health Organization, 2021.

What you can do

Do not put electronics in the normal bin.

Use a collection point on the Drop Points page, or the society collection.

Erase your data before you hand anything over.

This supports UN Goal 12

Target 12.4 is about handling waste and hazardous materials safely.
